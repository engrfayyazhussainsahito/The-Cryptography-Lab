Domain 01 // Foundations Security: 0-bit Classical (Cryptographically Broken)

01. Classical & Historical Ciphers

The foundation of cryptology began with pen-and-paper transposition and substitution, evolving into electromechanical rotor machinery. While completely insecure against modern computational cryptanalysis, these systems introduced essential concepts: substitution-permutation, diffusion, key space, and Claude Shannon's theorem of Perfect Secrecy.

Monoalphabetic & Polyalphabetic Substitution

Caesar Cipher: A monoalphabetic shift cipher mapping each character $p$ to $c \equiv (p + k) \pmod{26}$. With a trivial key space of $|\mathcal{K}| = 26$, it can be broken in microseconds via brute force or by comparing the unigram frequency distribution against standard language letter frequencies (e.g. 'E' at 12.7%, 'T' at 9.1%).

Atbash Cipher: A biblical Hebrew reciprocal cipher mapping letter index $x$ to $25 - x$. Because it requires no secret key, it provides zero confidentiality under Kerckhoffs's principle.

Vigenère Cipher: A polyalphabetic substitution cipher employing a keyword to shift letters according to a tabula recta: $c_i \equiv (p_i + k_{i \pmod L}) \pmod{26}$. Although long considered le chiffre indéchiffrable, it is broken via:

  • Kasiski Examination: Measuring the greatest common divisor (GCD) of distances between repeated ciphertext trigrams to deduce keyword length $L$.
  • Index of Coincidence (Friedman test): Computing $IC = \sum \frac{f_i(f_i - 1)}{N(N - 1)}$; English plaintext exhibits $IC \approx 0.0667$, while uniform random ciphertext exhibits $IC \approx 0.0385$.

Playfair Cipher: The first practical digraph substitution cipher, mapping pairs of letters onto a 5x5 Polybius square (combining 'I' and 'J'). While it flattens the single-letter frequency distribution, it remains vulnerable to bigram frequency cryptanalysis.

Hill Cipher: The first block cipher utilizing linear algebra over $\mathbb{Z}_{26}$. Plaintext vectors $\mathbf{p} = (p_1, p_2)^T$ are multiplied by an invertible $2 \times 2$ matrix $\mathbf{K}$:

$$\mathbf{c} \equiv \mathbf{K} \cdot \mathbf{p} \pmod{26}, \quad \text{where } \gcd(\det(\mathbf{K}), 26) = 1$$
Cryptanalytic Vulnerability // Known-Plaintext Attack (KPA)

Because the Hill cipher is entirely linear, an adversary who intercepts two plaintext-ciphertext digraph pairs can form $\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{K} \cdot \mathbf{P} \pmod{26}$ and invert the matrix $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{C} \cdot \mathbf{P}^{-1} \pmod{26}$ in constant time, entirely recovering the secret key!

Electromechanical Rotor Machines: Enigma & Lorenz SZ42

During WWII, mechanical polyalphabetic rotor machines drastically expanded the period before the substitution pattern repeated. The German Enigma utilized 3 wired rotors, an interchangeable reflector (Umkehrwalze), and a plugboard (Steckerbrett). The rotors stepped cyclically with every keystroke, yielding an effective key space of over $1.5 \times 10^{20}$ states.

The Fatal Enigma Flaw: The reflector routed electrical current back through the rotors, meaning a letter could never encrypt to itself ($E(x) \ne x$). Marian Rejewski and Alan Turing exploited this mathematical defect inside electromechanical Bombes to eliminate millions of false rotor configurations simultaneously.

Lorenz SZ42: A 12-wheel Vernam teleprinter stream cipher used by the German High Command. Cracked by Bill Tutte through statistical pseudo-random biases, leading to the construction of Colossus, the world's first programmable electronic digital computer.

The One-Time Pad (OTP) & Shannon's Perfect Secrecy

In 1949, Claude Shannon proved that the One-Time Pad (Vernam cipher) is the only provably unbreakable cipher in existence:

$$c_i = m_i \oplus k_i, \quad \text{where } H(M \mid C) = H(M)$$
Cryptanalytic Catastrophe // Two-Time Pad Attack ($C_1 \oplus C_2$)

If an identical key $K$ is ever used to encrypt two distinct messages $M_1$ and $M_2$, an eavesdropper computes: $$C_1 \oplus C_2 = (M_1 \oplus K) \oplus (M_2 \oplus K) = M_1 \oplus M_2$$ The key is completely eliminated. Through crib-dragging, both plaintexts are reconstructed. This fatal error exposed thousands of Soviet cables in the famous Venona project.

Domain 02 // Workhorse Security: 128 to 256-bit Classical // Grover 128-bit Post-Quantum

02. Symmetric Encryption: Block, Stream & Modes

Symmetric cryptography uses a shared secret key for both encryption and decryption. Block ciphers operate on fixed-size blocks via Substitution-Permutation Networks (SPN) or Feistel networks, while stream ciphers generate continuous pseudorandom keystreams. Mode selection dictates confidentiality, semantic security, and resilience to pattern leakage.

Block Ciphers: Architectures & Standards

DES & 3DES: Designed by IBM/NSA in 1977, DES is a 16-round Feistel network with a 64-bit block size. Its 56-bit effective key space was broken in 1998 in 56 hours. Double-DES ($2\text{DES}$) failed due to the Meet-in-the-Middle (MitM) attack. Triple-DES ($3\text{DES}$) offered 112 or 168-bit security, but its 64-bit block size makes it vulnerable to birthday collision attacks (Sweet32, CVE-2016-2183) after $2^{32}$ blocks ($\approx 32\text{ GB}$).

AES (Advanced Encryption Standard / Rijndael, FIPS 197): Selected by NIST in 2001, AES is an SPN cipher operating on a 128-bit state matrix ($4 \times 4$ bytes) with key sizes of 128 bits (10 rounds), 192 bits (12 rounds), or 256 bits (14 rounds). Transformations:

  • SubBytes: Non-linear byte substitution using the Rijndael S-box over $GF(2^8)$.
  • ShiftRows: Cyclical left byte shifts by offsets $[0, 1, 2, 3]$ to diffuse data across columns.
  • MixColumns: Matrix multiplication over $GF(2^8)$ modulo irreducible polynomial $x^8 + x^4 + x^3 + x + 1$ (0x11B).
  • AddRoundKey: Bitwise XOR of the state with the round subkey.
Twofish & Serpent AES Finalists
Twofish uses key-dependent S-boxes. Serpent uses 32 rounds of 4-bit S-boxes with conservative security margins.
Camellia & IDEA ISO / OpenSSL
Camellia is an international standard equivalent to AES. IDEA mixes operations from three incompatible algebraic groups.
SM4 & Kuznyechik National Standards
SM4 is China's official 128-bit block cipher standard. Kuznyechik is Russia's 128-bit SPN block cipher (GOST R 34.12-2015).

Stream Ciphers: ARX & Hardware Standards

RC4: Completely broken due to keystream biases in initial bytes (Fluhrer-Mantin-Shamir attack), destroying WEP and banned in TLS (RFC 7465).

ChaCha20 (RFC 8439): Designed by Daniel J. Bernstein. ChaCha20 uses an Add-Rotate-Xor (ARX) engine operating on a $4 \times 4$ matrix of 32-bit words (512-bit state). Completely immune to cache-timing side-channel attacks without requiring dedicated hardware instructions.

Cipher Operational Modes & The "ECB Penguin"

  • ECB (Electronic Codebook): Encrypts each block independently. Leaks structural patterns because identical plaintext blocks yield identical ciphertext blocks.
  • CBC (Cipher Block Chaining): XORs each plaintext block with previous ciphertext ($C_i = E_K(P_i \oplus C_{i-1})$). Vulnerable to padding oracle attacks without authentication.
  • CTR (Counter Mode): Evaluates keystream by encrypting $(Nonce \parallel Counter)$. Parallelizable in SIMD; never reuse a Nonce.
  • XTS (IEEE 1619): Tweakable block cipher mode for disk encryption (BitLocker, LUKS) preventing sector relocation attacks.
  • Format-Preserving Encryption (FF1, NIST SP 800-38G): Retains length and character set of structured data (e.g. credit cards).

Domain 03 // Authenticity Security: IND-CCA2 Authenticated (Tamper Proof)

03. Authenticated Encryption (AEAD) & MACs

Encryption alone guarantees confidentiality, but zero integrity. Without cryptographic authentication, ciphertexts are malleable: an active man-in-the-middle can flip bits, reorder packets, and craft chosen-ciphertext attacks (CCA). Modern security mandates Authenticated Encryption with Associated Data (AEAD).

The Doom Principle & AEAD Paradigms

In 2011, Moxie Marlinspike formulated the Cryptographic Doom Principle:

"If you have to perform any cryptographic parsing or state-machine actions on input data prior to verifying its message authentication tag, you will inevitably fall to doom."
  • MAC-then-Encrypt: Decodes and parses padding before verifying MAC, vulnerable to padding oracle attacks.
  • Encrypt-then-MAC (Modern Standard): Verifies MAC in constant-time over $(Ciphertext \parallel AssociatedData)$ before attempting any decryption.

Leading AEAD Algorithms & The Nonce-Reuse Trap

AES-GCM (NIST SP 800-38D): Evaluates GHASH over $GF(2^{128})$ with authentication subkey $H = E_K(0^{128})$. If a 96-bit nonce is ever reused, an attacker recovers $H$ and can forge tags for arbitrary packets.

ChaCha20-Poly1305 (RFC 8439): Combines ChaCha20 with Poly1305 modulo prime $2^{130}-5$.

XChaCha20-Poly1305: Extends nonce to 192 bits using HChaCha20, eliminating nonce collision concerns with random nonces.

Message Authentication Codes (MACs)

$$\text{HMAC}(K, M) = H\big((K \oplus opad) \parallel H((K \oplus ipad) \parallel M)\big)$$

SipHash protects hash tables against collision-flooding DoS attacks.

Domain 04 // One-Way Digests Properties: Preimage · Second Preimage · Collision Resistance

04. Cryptographic Hashes, XOFs & Algebraic Proof Hashes

Cryptographic hash functions map arbitrary-length inputs to fixed-size bitstrings, satisfying Preimage Resistance ($O(2^n)$), Second Preimage Resistance ($O(2^n)$), and Collision Resistance ($O(2^{n/2})$). Modern applications demand Extendable-Output Functions (XOFs) and algebraic hashes for Zero-Knowledge Proofs.

The Merkle–Damgård Construction & Broken Hashes

MD5: Broken via collision attacks in 2004. Flame malware utilized MD5 collisions to forge code signatures.

SHA-1: Broken in 2017 via the Google SHAttered collision attack ($2^{63.1}$ computations).

SHA-2: Secure standard; however, Merkle–Damgård hashes suffer from Length Extension attacks.

The Sponge Construction (SHA-3 / Keccak) & BLAKE3

SHA-3 (FIPS 202): Uses sponge state of 1600 bits divided into Rate $r$ and Capacity $c$. Immune to length extension by design. XOFs (SHAKE128/256) permit arbitrary output lengths.

BLAKE3: Tree-based SIMD parallel cryptographic hash producing digests at $>15\text{ GB/s}$.

Algebraic Hashes for Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs)

Standard hashes require thousands of R1CS constraints per compression. Poseidon, Rescue, and Anemoi operate natively over prime fields $\mathbb{F}_p$ using low-degree power maps ($S(x) = x^5 \pmod p$), speeding up zk-SNARK provers by up to 100×.

Domain 05 // Password Defense Architecture: Memory-Hard Functions & Zero-Leakage PAKE

05. Key Derivation & Password-Authenticated Key Exchange

Human passwords have low entropy. Standard hashes are inadequate because GPU/ASIC clusters compute billions of hashes per second. Secure systems require memory-hard Password Hashing Functions and Password-Authenticated Key Exchange (PAKE).

Memory-Hard Password Hashing: Argon2id

PBKDF2 requires minimal RAM and is vulnerable to ASIC parallelism. bcrypt's 4 KB state fits in modern GPU L1 cache.

Argon2id (RFC 9106): Winner of the Password Hashing Competition. Utilizes memory-hard operations bottlenecked by memory bandwidth, neutralizing GPU clusters.

HKDF & Zero-Knowledge PAKE

HKDF (RFC 5869): Extract-then-Expand key derivation.

OPAQUE PAKE: Evaluates an Oblivious PRF (OPRF). Even if the server database is compromised, offline dictionary attacks are mathematically impossible without the server's private key.

Domain 06 // Number Theory Threat Notice: Shor's Quantum Algorithm Breaks in Polynomial Time

06. Classical Public-Key Cryptography & The Quantum Threat

Public-key cryptography enables key exchange over untrusted channels without a pre-shared secret, relying on Integer Factorization and Discrete Logarithms. Shor's quantum algorithm completely breaks all factoring and discrete-log schemes.

RSA, Diffie-Hellman & Elliptic Curves

$$c \equiv m^e \pmod n, \quad m \equiv c^d \pmod n, \quad \text{where } e \cdot d \equiv 1 \pmod{\phi(n)}$$

Curves: NIST P-256, secp256k1 (Bitcoin), Curve25519 (X25519 / Ed25519), and pairing curves (BLS12-381).

Cryptanalytic Catastrophe // ECDSA Nonce-Reuse Attack

If an identical nonce $k$ is used to sign two distinct messages, an attacker recovers $k = \frac{z_1 - z_2}{s_1 - s_2} \pmod n$ and extracts private key $d$ in $O(1)$ arithmetic (Sony PS3 exploit).

Shor's Quantum Algorithm

Peter Shor's 1994 quantum algorithm factors integers and computes discrete logarithms in $O((\log N)^3)$ polynomial time using the Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT), rendering RSA, ECC, and DH insecure against a Cryptanalytically Relevant Quantum Computer.

Domain 07 // The New Era Official Standards: NIST FIPS 203 · 204 · 205 (August 2024 / 2025)

07. Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC)

Post-Quantum Cryptography refers to classical algorithms running on existing conventional hardware designed to resist quantum attacks. In August 2024, NIST released FIPS 203, FIPS 204, and FIPS 205, followed by FN-DSA and HQC selection in 2025.

The Official NIST Standards (FIPS 203, 204, 205, 206)

ML-KEM (Kyber, FIPS 203): Primary global standard for key encapsulation based on Module-LWE.

ML-DSA (Dilithium, FIPS 204): Primary lattice-based digital signature standard.

SLH-DSA (SPHINCS+, FIPS 205): Stateless hash-based signatures providing mathematical hedge against lattice breaks.

HQC (Hamming Quasi-Cyclic): Selected by NIST in 2025 as the secondary general-purpose backup KEM.

Broken PQC Post-Mortem & Hybrid Deployments

SIKE Break (2022)

Castryck & Decru used auxiliary torsion points to reconstruct secret isogenies in under 1 hour on a single CPU.

Rainbow Break (2022)

Ward Beullens' MinRank intersection attack broke Rainbow Level 1 in 53 hours.

Hybrid Deployments: X25519MLKEM768 combines classical ECDH and ML-KEM via HKDF for defense-in-depth.

Domain 08 // Physics-Based Security Security: Unconditional Information-Theoretic (Heisenberg & No-Cloning)

08. Quantum Cryptography Proper & Quantum Key Distribution

Quantum Cryptography uses physical laws of nature (Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle, the No-Cloning Theorem, and Quantum Entanglement) to build communication channels with unconditional information-theoretic security.

BB84 & E91 Entanglement QKD

BB84 (Bennett & Brassard 1984): Photons transmitted across Rectilinear ($+$) and Diagonal ($\times$) bases. Eavesdropping introduces a Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER) of ~25%.

E91 (Ekert 1991): Uses entangled photon pairs $|\Phi^+\rangle$ and evaluates the CHSH inequality ($S \le 2\sqrt{2}$). Eavesdropping forces $S \le 2$, immediately exposing Eve.

Domain 09 // Next-Gen Privacy Paradigm: Verifiable Computation & Computation on Encrypted Data

09. Advanced & Privacy-Enhancing Cryptography (PETs)

Privacy-Enhancing Technologies enable proving statements without revealing information (Zero-Knowledge Proofs), computing directly on encrypted ciphertexts (Fully Homomorphic Encryption), and executing multi-party computations with zero mutual trust.

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs)

Properties: Completeness, Soundness, and Zero-Knowledge. Includes zk-SNARKs (Groth16, PLONK, Halo2), zk-STARKs (FRI, transparent post-quantum), and folding schemes (Nova).

Homomorphic Encryption & Shamir Secret Sharing

Paillier PHE: Additively homomorphic ($c_1 \cdot c_2 \pmod{n^2} = E(m_1 + m_2)$).

Shamir $(k, n)$: Splits secret $S$ into polynomial shares. Any $k$ shares reconstruct $S$ via Lagrange interpolation.

Domain 10 // Real-World Systems Scope: TLS 1.3 · WireGuard · Signal Double Ratchet · FIDO2

10. Security Protocols & Cryptographic Systems

Protocols compose primitives into state machines resilient against active adversaries, session hijacking, replay attacks, and endpoint compromises.

TLS 1.3, WireGuard & Signal Double Ratchet

TLS 1.3 (RFC 8446): 1-RTT handshake, mandatory forward secrecy, and Encrypted Client Hello (ECH).

Signal Double Ratchet: Symmetric KDF chain ratchet advances per message (Forward Secrecy); asymmetric DH ratchet advances per reply (Post-Compromise Security healing).

FIDO2 / WebAuthn & Passkeys: Asymmetric phishing-resistant web authentication.

11. Lightweight Cryptography for IoT & Embedded Systems

Billions of IoT microcontrollers, RFID tags, automotive CAN buses, and medical implants operate under milliwatt budgets where standard AES-256 consumes excessive area and power.

NIST SP 800-232: Ascon (Finalized 2025)

Operates a sponge duplex mode over a 320-bit state ($5 \times 64$-bit words $x_0, \dots, x_4$) using Round Constants ($p_C$), a 5-bit bit-sliced S-box ($p_S$), and linear 64-bit diffusion rotations ($p_L$).
